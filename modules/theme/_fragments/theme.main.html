How the storefront looks. Publishing takes effect for every visitor straight away — nothing is redeployed.

Storefront theme

One theme, everyone. Publishing changes the shop for every visitor — there is no per-visitor setting.
Luxe adds movement — a drifting background, a sheen across cards and buttons, rules that draw in as sections arrive. A visitor who has asked their device to reduce motion gets the colours and none of the movement, automatically.
No theme changes a price, a product or a page. Switching back is the same two clicks.
A theme is what the shop is wearing. How its home page is arranged — which sections move, which stand still, and differently on a phone than on a computer — is the Home layout screen.

No backend connected. The shop is serving the theme that was built into its pages, and this screen cannot change that for visitors — a universal setting needs somewhere central to store it.
Preview still works, and it is safe: it only changes the shop in the tab it opens. To change it for everyone on a static deployment, rebuild with python tools/assemble.py --theme luxe and deploy.

Mini cart

The cart that slides in from the right when something is added. Its footer offers two ways on: View Cart, which opens the full cart page, and Checkout.

Switched off, the drawer offers Checkout alone — the shorter path, and the right one for a shop where baskets are two or three lines and the cart page is a detour. The button is only hidden: nothing is deleted, the cart page is still reachable from the header and the footer, and turning this back on brings it straight back.
Checkout itself, the tick beside each line and the remove button cannot be switched off. The first is the sale; the other two are how a shopper says what they are buying today and what they are keeping for later.

No backend connected. Every visitor sees the drawer as it ships — with the button — and this switch cannot change that until there is somewhere central to store it.
